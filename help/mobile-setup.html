Setting up Cora Mobile

On Cora Max: Setting up Cora Max

This page takes you through your first ten minutes with Cora Mobile, in the order the screens appear. When you're done, you'll have an account, a tank and a dashboard with readings on it.

Install Cora Mobile

On iPhone, get Cora Mobile from the App Store. On Android, Cora Mobile is coming soon to Google Play. The icon on your home screen says Cora. That's Cora Mobile.

The welcome screens

The first time you open Cora Mobile, a few short screens introduce it. Tap Next to go through them, or Skip at the top right to go straight to sign-in.

Two of them ask for something:

  • Your devices live on your Wi-Fi warns you about a question your iPhone asks later. When you add your first piece of equipment, iPhone asks whether Cora may find devices on your local network. Allow it, or Cora can't find anything on your Wi-Fi. If you said no, Settings → Device access takes you to the switch. See Settings.
  • The last screen is about alerts. Tap Allow notifications, then allow it in your phone's prompt, so alerts reach this phone. Maybe later skips it. More in Notifications.

Looking around first

You don't need an account to look around. On the sign-in screen, tap Explore a demo reef. It's a sample tank with realistic data, laid out like a real one. No equipment is involved, and nothing you do there is saved.

When you're ready for your own tank, tap Make it yours.

Create your account

  1. On the sign-in screen, tap Sign Up (under Don't have an account?).
  2. Enter your email address and a password, twice. The password needs at least 8 characters, an uppercase letter, a number and a symbol. The list under Your password needs: ticks off as you type.
  3. Tick I agree to: Terms and Conditions and Privacy Policy. Both are links, so you can read them first.
  4. Tap Create Account.

You can also tap Continue with Apple or Continue with Google and skip the password. Tick the terms box there too. With Apple, choose Share My Email when Apple asks, so lab and ICP results can reach your Cora account.

If you signed up with an email address, Cora now asks you to verify it. Open the email from Cora on the same phone and tap Verify my email. Then come back to Cora Mobile and tap I've Verified My Email. If nothing arrives within a minute, check your spam folder, or tap Resend verification email. More in Signing in and account recovery.

One account for every screen

Your account holds everything. Pair a Cora Max with it, or sign in on another phone, and you'll see the same tanks, devices, readings and records.

Your phone dashboard layout follows the account to any phone you sign in on. Each Cora Max keeps its own layout. See What is shared and what is not.

Add your first tank

In Cora, a tank is any body of water you want to track. Most people have one. If you run a frag system or a quarantine tank, each of those is a tank of its own.

After you sign in, Let's meet your tank asks three things. Only the name is required, and you can change all of it later in your tank profile.

  1. What do you call it? is the name you use day to day, like "Display", "Frag" or "QT".
  2. What kind of tank is it? is SPS Reef, LPS Reef, Mixed Reef, Soft Coral Reef, FOWLR or Fish Only. The type sets your starting alert ranges. If you're unsure, pick Mixed Reef.
  3. How much water? is the real water volume, sump included. Dosing maths uses this number, so try to get it roughly right. If you only know the size of the tank, tap Don't know the volume? Enter dimensions and Cora works it out.

Tap Create my tank.

Is your tank still cycling?

Tap Starting a new tank? on the dashboard. That card shows while the tank has no readings. If you have already logged a test, open the tank's profile and tap the same card at the top: it works at any time. More in Guided journeys.

Cora also judges readings against what's normal for a tank of that age. The wizard doesn't ask for it, so set Tank start date in your tank profile, under Tank Dimensions.

"Your tank is live"

Next you'll see your tank's name with is live, for example "Display" is live. It offers four next steps. Pick any of them, or none.

  • Set up my tank, guided walks you through the rest of the profile, one section at a time. See Guided setup. It ends with Add devices, which takes you to adding your equipment.
  • Connect your gear pairs a Cora Max. For a Neptune™ Apex, Red Sea™ or any other brand, use Devices → Add Device instead (see below).
  • Log your first test opens the first-test step described in The first-test card.
  • Ask the assistant anything opens Cora Assistant with a first question ready to send.

When you're done, tap Go to my dashboard, or I'll do this later.

Your first 30 days

On a new free account, Go to my dashboard first shows Your first 30 days are on us. Your 30-day Advanced trial started when you created your account, and no card is needed. Tap Start exploring to carry on, or See plans to look at the paid plans. More in Plans and subscriptions.

Your first look at the dashboard

Two things happen the first time you open the dashboard.

  • Cora asks Enable Personalized Reef Guidance? Tap Enable if you want Cora Assistant to use your tank's saved data in its answers. Not Now leaves it off. More in Asking Cora.
  • A short tour points out the main parts of the screen. See The first-run tour.

Connect your equipment

Go to the Devices tab, tap Add Device and pick the brand. Cora works with gear you already own. Connecting your equipment lists what's supported and what each device needs.

You can skip this and come back later. A tank works fine without devices. You just log readings by hand.

Log your first readings

Some parameters only come from a test kit. Scroll down the dashboard and tap Log Parameters, in the row of buttons under the widgets.

Cora saves each reading with its source and time. That's how it can tell you when a probe and a test kit disagree.

The first-test card

A tank with no readings and no linked equipment shows a card under the widgets, just above Log Parameters. It says Log your first water test. One reading is enough for Reef Buddy, alerts and insights to start working. Tap the card and choose one of these.

  • Log test results now opens the Log Parameters form.
  • Link a device (Apex or Red Sea) opens the pairing screen for a Cora Max. To link an Apex or a Red Sea unit, go to Devices → Add Device and pick the brand. If your tank profile already lists a device Cora can link, the card names it, for example Link your Neptune Apex.
  • Later closes the sheet.
  • Remind me to test every week adds a weekly maintenance task called Test water. Pick the Test day first.

Tap the cross on the card to hide it. The card goes away by itself once the tank has a reading or a linked source. Cora Max shows Log your first test on a tank with no data, too.

If a tank still has no readings three days after you create it, Cora sends one reminder push. If none of your tanks has any readings, Cora also sends a short email on day 3 and day 10 after you signed up, once each. Both follow your Maintenance Reminders switch in Settings → Notifications, and the email has a line saying how to stop it.

What good looks like

You're set up when the Dashboard shows your tank name at the top and a few widgets with numbers in them. A Reef Buddy card follows once the first briefing runs overnight.

Next, have a look at Finding your way around. To add a second tank, see Managing your tanks.
