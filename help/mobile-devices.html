Adding, editing and removing devices

On Cora Max: Devices and device health

On this page

  • Adding equipment
  • Pairing a Cora Max
  • A device's page
  • Renaming
  • Checking whether a device is healthy
  • Removing a device

The Devices tab is where you add, assign, rename, check and remove every piece of equipment. Devices are grouped by brand, and you can collapse each group.

Adding equipment

  1. On the Devices tab, tap Add Device.
  2. Pick the brand: Cora, Neptune™ Apex, Red Sea™, Jecod™ or AquaWiz™.
  3. Follow the brand's steps. Depending on the brand that's a network scan, an IP address, a sign-in or a device key. Connecting your equipment has the steps for each brand.
  4. Choose the tank it belongs to.

Without a Cora Max, some equipment is local only

Neptune Apex, Red Sea and Jecod equipment is reached over your home network or Bluetooth. Without a Cora Max, the add screen says This integration works only locally on the Mobile app without Cora Max. Your phone reads and controls it only while it's on the same network (or in Bluetooth range). Away from home you see the last readings your phone saved and can't control it. A Cora Max at the tank reads it around the clock and passes your commands on. More in Connecting your equipment.

When you add equipment from your network, your phone has to be on the same network as the equipment.

Bluetooth capacity

A Cora Max shares its Bluetooth between all the equipment it reads, so it can only keep up with so much. When you add a Jecod pump that connects over Bluetooth, Cora checks how busy the tank's Cora Max is first. Usually you see nothing. Otherwise one of two messages appears:

  • Bluetooth is getting busy: This Cora Max is close to its Bluetooth limit. More devices may update less often. Add a second Cora Max if you plan to add more. Tap Continue to add the device anyway, or Cancel.
  • Bluetooth limit reached: This Cora Max is at its Bluetooth limit. Add a second Cora Max for these devices. The device isn't added. Tap OK.

Cora checks the tank's primary Cora Max or, if none is chosen, the Cora Max with the most room. More in More than one Cora device.

Pairing a Cora Max

Cora is how you pair a new Cora Max. It finds units already on your Wi-Fi, or nearby ones over Bluetooth. If it can't find yours, use Enter IP Address Manually on the same screen. The full walk-through is in Setting up Cora Max.

A device's page

Tap any device in the list to open its page. Its controls come first, then sections that work the same way for every brand.

  • Status shows the Model, Serial, Firmware and when Cora last heard from it (Last seen).
  • Tanks shows which tank (or tanks) it's assigned to. Tap Change to reassign it.
  • Connection is where you edit its IP address, sign-in or device key.
  • Remove device, at the bottom.

Cora Max and Neptune Apex can serve more than one tank, so their tank picker is a checklist. Cora Max can be assigned up to four. See More than one Cora device. Everything else serves one tank at a time. Picking a different one moves the device there and takes it off the old one.

Assign the tank before you rely on the readings

A device without a tank still reports, but its numbers have nowhere to go. If a device you just added isn't showing on a dashboard, check this first.

What each brand's controls do is in Controlling your equipment.

Renaming

Open the device and tap the pencil beside its name. Use what you call it day to day, like "Return", "Left gyre" or "Sump heater". The name shows up on widgets, in alerts and in anything you ask Cora.

Names can be up to 40 characters. Leave the name empty to go back to the device's own name. If two devices of the same kind have no name, Cora tells them apart with a short code after the name. Cora never uses a serial number as a name.

The new name only applies in Cora. It doesn't change the name in the manufacturer's own app.

Checking whether a device is healthy

Each device is one row on the Devices tab, with its icon, name, model and tank. The dot on the row shows its state.

DotWhat it means
GreyCora doesn't know yet
GreenWorking normally
OrangeA warning or a fault
RedOffline

For more, open the device. Its Status section shows when Cora last heard from it. You want a recent time and no warning.

What you seeWhat it means
A recent update timeWorking normally
"Updated 3 h ago" on something that only reports every few hoursFine
"Could not reach…"A network problem, or the device is off
"…refused the sign-in"The manufacturer's account needs reconnecting. Open the device and sign in again
Older readings while you're awayNormal for Apex, Red Sea and Jecod without a Cora Max at the tank (see above)
Nothing at allIt's never reported. Check the tank assignment and the connection

When a device stops reporting, Cora can alert you. The timings are in Alerts and thresholds. For anything else, see Troubleshooting.

Removing a device

Open the device and tap Remove device, or swipe its row to the left on the Devices tab. Cora asks you to confirm: "{name} will be removed from Cora. It is not reset or changed on the device itself."

Your readings are kept. Removing a device stops Cora collecting new data from it. The history it already gathered stays on the tank, and any widget that used it keeps its past readings.

You lose the live link. If the device connected through a manufacturer account, you also lose the saved sign-in, so you'll need to sign in again if you add it back.

Quieten a noisy device without removing it

If a device works fine but alerts too often, change its thresholds or notification settings in Alerts and thresholds. You keep the connection and the data, and the noise stops.
