Guided journeys

On this page

  • Starting the cycling journey
  • Cycling a new tank
  • When the cycle is done
  • Working through an issue
  • Dismissing a journey

In a journey, Cora walks you through something over days or weeks. There are two kinds, and they work differently: cycling a new tank, and working through an issue.

Starting the cycling journey

Guided cycling is free on every plan. You start it from the tank's dashboard.

  1. Open the new tank on the Dashboard tab. A card asks Starting a new tank?
  2. Tap it. The first time, Cora explains Guided cycling: while the tank cycles, your dashboard focuses on ammonia, nitrite and nitrate and pauses the usual reef checks, which don't apply yet.
  3. Tap Got it: start cycling. The journey opens.

Start it before you log anything or link equipment

The Starting a new tank? card only shows on a tank with no readings at all. As soon as you log a test or link a device that sends readings, the card goes away, and there's no other way to start the cycling journey. If your tank is still cycling, start the journey first, then log your first test from inside it.

Once it's running, the dashboard card reads Continue cycling · Day N. Tap it to get back to the journey. If it says Resume cycling, the tank is still set up for cycling: tap it to reopen the guide.

Cycling a new tank

This is for a tank that isn't ready for livestock yet. Cycling grows the bacteria that turn toxic ammonia into safe nitrate, and the journey follows that process to the end.

The journey screen, New Tank: cycling, has Your setup checklist, with the steps for getting the tank ready and seeding it, and a short why and how for each. Tick them off as you go.

Tap Log a test to add an ammonia, nitrite and nitrate test. Enter what your kit read and leave the rest blank, then tap Save test. Test every two to three days. Cora works out your stage from those readings:

StageWhat is happening
Log your first testNothing logged yet. The journey needs numbers before it can place you
Ready to startEverything is near zero and there's no nitrate yet. Ammonia hasn't been added, or hasn't started converting
Ammonia stageThe first colony is growing and eating ammonia
Nitrite stage: about halfwayThe second colony is clearing nitrite. This is usually the longest stretch, and the one where people think something's gone wrong because the numbers stop moving
Looks cycledAmmonia and nitrite are both gone and nitrate is present. Do the final check on screen before you mark the tank as cycled

Your cycle so far plots the three curves together. They're scaled against each other instead of to their real values, so you can see one colony hand over to the next. Tap a name to see the real numbers.

For a second opinion, tap Ask Cora about cycling. It opens Cora Assistant with a question about your latest readings.

Two things stall a cycle

Ammonia or nitrite above 5 ppm stalls the bacteria you're trying to grow. If you overshoot, a partial water change brings it back down. pH under 7 stalls it too. The journey warns you if either happens, because waiting longer won't help.

Log all three tests

Cora only shows Looks cycled when ammonia and nitrite have each been measured at zero and nitrate is present. Nitrate alone isn't enough. Source water often has some, and on its own it could look like a finished cycle and tempt you to add livestock too early.

Readiness comes from your readings

The journey judges readiness on what you've logged. How many weeks have gone by doesn't count. If you haven't tested for a fortnight, the tank stays where it was. That's why it shows how long it's been since your last test.

When the cycle is done

When the journey shows Looks cycled, do the final check: dose a full ammonia load of about 2 ppm and test again about 24 hours later. If ammonia and nitrite both read zero with nitrate present, you're cycled.

  1. Tap Mark my tank as cycled.
  2. Cora asks Mark your tank as cycled? Tap Yes, cycled, or Not yet to keep going.

The tank switches to normal monitoring and the full reef intelligence turns on. Add livestock slowly, hardiest first.

Working through an issue

Issue journeys cover one problem: dinoflagellates. Cora offers one when your readings and your report point to it. The journey only starts if you accept. Cora never starts one on its own.

Only two journeys so far

Cycling and dinoflagellates are the only journeys for now. For other problems, use Cora Assistant and the journal.

An issue journey has three stages:

  1. Verify: can you trust the reading? Confirm it with a fresh test, then tap Confirmed: it's real. If it doesn't match your tank, tap It's not that / dismiss.
  2. Plan: Cora lists the steps, with why and how for each. Tick them off as you go, for as long as it takes, then tap How's it going?
  3. Outcome: did it work?

You complete Verify yourself

An issue journey opens on Verify. It asks you to confirm the reading before it shows a plan. A cycling journey has no Verify step. It works from the tests you log.

Recording the outcome

At the end, you say what happened:

Resolved · Improving · No change · Worse · Stopped

Be honest, even if it's Worse. The outcome is saved with the journey, so if a similar problem comes up later you can compare it with what you tried and how it went.

Dismissing a journey

If an issue journey isn't useful, dismiss it. It stops showing up, and Cora records the dismissal so it offers similar journeys less often.
