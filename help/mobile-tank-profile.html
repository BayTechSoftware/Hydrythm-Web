Your tank profile

On this page

  • Guided setup
  • Completeness
  • Sections
  • Units
  • Volume

  • Keeping the profile current
  • Adding another tank
  • Removing a tank

The tank profile describes your system. Cora uses it to judge readings, work out doses and decide what's normal for your tank in particular.

To open it, tap the pencil on the right of the tank header, or go to Settings, tap the tank and choose Edit tank. Tapping the tank name in the header is a quick rename. It doesn't open the profile.

Guided setup

There's a lot in a profile, so Guided setup takes you through it one section at a time. It opens from Set up my tank, guided right after you create a tank, from Settings → your tank → Guided setup, and from the profile prompt on the dashboard.

The sections come in this order: Tank Name & Type, Tank Dimensions, Lighting, Dosing, Water Management, Livestock, Temperature Control, Flow & Filtration and Pest & Treatment. The top of the screen shows Step 2 of 10 and so on.

  • Save & continue saves the section and moves on. Skip moves on without saving it.
  • Finish later takes you back and keeps the answers you've saved so far. When you open guided setup again, it picks up where you stopped.
  • If you leave a section with unsaved changes, Cora asks Leave this step? Tap Keep editing or Leave.

If you haven't answered it yet, the last page is Cora Assistant & your data. Turn on Use my tank data for personalized advice if you want Cora Assistant's answers to use this profile, then tap Save, or tap Decide later. You can change it any time in Settings. See Asking Cora.

At the end you'll see Nice work: your tank profile is set up. Add devices takes you to the Devices tab to add equipment for this tank, and Go to my dashboard takes you back.

Each section changes what Cora can tell you

The more you fill in, the more Cora can do. Dosing products make the dose calculator and consumption tracking work. Livestock and tank type change what your readings are judged against. Equipment tells Cora what you have. With a thin profile, you'll get more general answers.

Completeness

The percentage at the top of the profile shows how much of it you've filled in. Tap it to jump to the next section that isn't finished. The fuller the profile, the more specific the advice. With an empty one, Cora falls back on defaults.

The dashboard shows a prompt to finish the profile while it's under 80% complete. It opens guided setup until you've been through it once, and the profile after that. Close the prompt and it stays away until you next open Cora Mobile.

Sections

In the profile, the sections are listed like this. Tap one to open it. The info icon next to each one tells you what its fields are used for.

SectionCovers
Feeding routineHow you usually feed, so you only log the exceptions. See The journal
Tank Name & TypeWhat you call the tank and its type
Tank DimensionsPhysical size, total water volume and the tank's start date
DosingWhat you dose and how
Water ManagementWater changes, top-off, salinity targets
LivestockWhat the tank holds and how heavily it is stocked
Temperature ControlHeating and cooling equipment
LightingFixtures and photoperiod
Flow & FiltrationPumps, skimming, media and your controller's brand and model
Parameter SourcesWhich source each parameter is read from
Pest & TreatmentPests you've dealt with, their status and what you tried

When you add a heater, pump, light or skimmer under Temperature Control, Flow & Filtration or Lighting, Cora suggests brands and models as you type. If yours isn't on the list, type it in anyway. Cora keeps whatever you enter.

Parameter Sources isn't shown while you're adding a new tank. It covers the same choice as Parameter Mapping in the tank's settings, which gives you full control over every probe. See Parameter Mapping.

Devices live on the Devices tab

Your Apex, Red Sea™ units and every other device are added, assigned and removed from the Devices tab, not from the profile. See Adding, editing and removing devices. The tank's page in Settings shows a read-only Devices on this tank list if you just want to check what's assigned.

Units

Each tank has its own display units. Tap the ruler icon in the tank header, or go to Settings, tap the tank and choose Units. You can set how this tank shows salinity, temperature, alkalinity, volume, phosphate and nitrate. Volume can be litres, US gallons or UK gallons.

The units apply to this tank on every phone and Cora Max that opens it. Your readings aren't changed, only how they're shown. Gauges, tiles, history, reports and what Cora Assistant says all follow the choice. On Cora Max, open the tank menu and tap Units.

Volume

Enter the real water volume, sump included. Don't use the display volume printed on the tank.

Doses scale directly with volume

If the volume is 20% too high, dose suggestions are 20% too high as well. If you're not sure, measure it. Don't guess.

Tank age

Set Tank start date under Tank Dimensions. Cora judges readings against what's normal for a tank that age, so a three-month-old system and a five-year-old one are judged differently.

If the tank is still cycling, tap the Starting a new tank? card on the dashboard to start guided cycling. Do it before you log the first test or link equipment, because the card only shows on a tank with no readings at all. More in Guided journeys.

Keeping the profile current

Update the profile when your system changes, for example new equipment, different stocking or a new dosing routine. Cora works from the profile, so if it's out of date, the advice will be too.

Adding another tank

Go to Settings and tap Add tank. It opens this same profile, empty, with Save Tank at the bottom. The full steps, including equipment and Cora Max, are in Managing your tanks.

Removing a tank

Remove Tank is at the bottom of the profile. It hides the tank and keeps its data, and you can bring it back later. Before you use it, read Removing a tank, because some things keep running.
